Interactive companion dataset

Lithium Polysulfide Speciation in Lithium–Sulfur Batteries

A First-Principles Benchmark of Thermodynamics, Bonding, and Solvent Control

This site lets you browse and download every dataset underlying the paper: optimized molecular geometries, M06-2X and composite-CCSD energies, QTAIM and NBO bonding descriptors, and the harmonic IR and Raman spectra of every species, with an interactive spectrum viewer, for the full LiySxq matrix in two electrolyte solvents. It runs entirely in your browser with no server and no network access; the underlying files can all be downloaded from the Data & Citation tab.

Abstract

Lithium–sulfur cycle life is limited by dissolved LiySxq polysulfides whose speciation remains disputed. We benchmark the complete matrix (y = 0–2, q = 0 to −2, x = 1–8) in the gas phase, tetraglyme and dimethyl sulfoxide: 248 M06-2X/SMD thermochemistries, 132 refined by single-point CCSD. The convex hull, S8 → Li2S6 → Li2S4 → Li2S3 → Li2S2 → Li2S, is identical in both solvents, and every exergonic long-chain comproportionation delivers Li2S6. QTAIM analysis of the CCSD densities separates covalent S–S from electrostatic Li–S bonding, transferably between solvents. Solvation makes Sx2− the dominant lithium-free charge state and places both legs of the shuttle on that branch. Infrared and Raman spectra of prepared solutions test the vibrational predictions and track the chain-length distribution through one band envelope. The open dataset is a calibrated reference for force-field, machine-learned and continuum battery models.

How the data is organised

The paper distinguishes a main species set (Li2Sx neutrals, LiSx−, and the full Sx0/•−/2− block) from an SI set (the Li2Sx•− and Li2Sx2− anions and neutral LiSx• radicals), because a bare continuum over-stabilises multiply charged solutes. Both sets are exposed here and labelled throughout. Of the 248 thermochemistries, 202 enter the analysis (the 41 exploratory x ≥ 9 rows, one duplicate row and four electrolyte rows computed outside their own medium do not) and 132 carry a composite-CCSD refinement. A species is a radical when nLi + |q| is odd, and is written with a dot (Sx•−, Li2Sx•−, LiSx•).

Flagged entries. Data points that failed a validation check are retained with their diagnostic and marked wherever they appear († in the tables here, as in the paper; the caveats section of the paper's Methods and Supplementary Note 12 of its Supplementary Information, SI):
  • neutral LiS3• in DMSO is not a minimum: the optimization terminated on a C2v first-order saddle point with one imaginary mode at 60i cm−1, and no minimum was located; its Gibbs energy is an upper bound, high by about 0.03 eV from the missing mode;
  • Li2S32− in DMSO is a suspect energy: its raw free energy lies above that of the monoanion, inverting the TEGDME ordering; it is excluded from the redox-ladder figure and statistics;
  • LiS5− in TEGDME is monodentate where every comparable species is bidentate and sits 0.9 eV above its own ladder; possibly a genuine coordination difference, so it is retained.
Two further points are notes rather than flags. The reference S8 geometry is the D4d crown (0.42 eV below the Cs exo-endo ring at CCSD), and every number here uses it. The QTAIM densities of Li2S2− (both solvents) and Li2S32− (DMSO) contain a non-nuclear attractor, a basin holding 1.07–1.65 e; with it counted, all 131 systems conserve charge, so it is reported as a finding on the diffuse dianion density. The triplet S atom and S2, once run as singlets, were recomputed as triplets and are included normally.

Energies & Thermodynamics

  
Formation Gibbs energies ΔGform at M06-2X and composite-CCSD levels, referenced
  in-solvent to Li, Li+, and S8 (paper Eq. 2). The CCSD−DFT column is the deviation
  inherited by downstream users; positive means DFT over-stabilisation (the paper's deviation, M06-2X − CCSD,
  is its negative). Over the 128 benchmarked (M06-2X, CCSD) pairs, 64 per solvent, the mean absolute deviation is
  0.189 eV in DMSO and 0.191 eV in TEGDME; the Li2Sx ladders are biased by −0.30 eV in the
  paper's convention (M06-2X − CCSD, i.e. +0.30 eV in the CCSD−DFT column here) and the
  Sx2− dianions agree to 0.03 eV.

Discharge voltages

  
Average voltage of the 16-electron conversion S8 + 16 Li → 8 Li2S (V),
  computed from the tabulated Gibbs energies with the paper's own code (paper Table 2). The
  bulk-lithium frame is the physically correct anode reference; the experimental cell voltage
  is 2.30 V. The 1.2–1.3 V shortfall is the lattice stabilisation of crystalline Li2S, which a
  solution-phase molecular model deliberately omits, not an electronic-structure error. The atomic frame sits
  1.25 V higher (the solvation free energy of the Li atom plus the bulk cohesive energy); its proximity to
  experiment is partly a cancellation and is not presented as agreement.

  







  
Bonding & Topology (QTAIM)

  
Quantum Theory of Atoms in Molecules analysis of the CCSD relaxed densities in both
  solvents: 777 bond critical points (383
  in DMSO, 394 in TEGDME) across
  122 solvated systems. Covalent S–S bonds (shared-shell,
  ∇²ρ < 0, H < 0) and electrostatic Li–S contacts (closed-shell) occupy disjoint regions
  with an empty transit region; secondary S···S contacts are separated from the covalent bonds by a 0.76 Å gap
  in distance. Terminal sulfurs carry −0.62 e against −0.12 e for internal ones, and the matched descriptors
  agree between the solvents to 0.015 Å in distance and 0.0016 a.u. in density.

NBO donor→acceptor channels

  
Second-order perturbation energies E(2) (kcal/mol) by element channel and
  solvent (NBO 3.1 on the M06-2X/def2-TZVPPD/SMD wavefunctions; 591 polysulfide interactions over
  69 species; the TFSI− reference rows are in the CSV
  files but not in this table, which matches Supplementary Table 34 of the paper). Lithium
  participates almost exclusively as an acceptor: sulfur lone pairs donate into empty Li orbitals with a median
  near 13–14 kcal/mol in both solvents, the modest dative character of an essentially ionic contact. No Li–S
  bonding orbital exists in any main-set species; weak Li–S bonding orbitals appear only in three reduced
  SI-set anions (Supplementary Note 11). The S8 rows are those of the
  D4d crown in both solvents; each molecule
  contributes every interaction of at least half its largest E(2).

  







  
Vibrational Spectra

  
Harmonic IR and Raman spectra (M06-2X/def2-TZVPPD/SMD) of all 37 solution species in both
  solvents, with the two solvent molecules and the TFSI− counter-ion as interference references:
  977 normal modes in all. Frequencies follow the paper's two-domain convention.
  The solvent and TFSI− modes are scaled by 0.96 (the least-squares fit to 19 measured neat-solvent
  infrared bands gives 0.963, mean absolute deviation 7 cm−1); the polysulfide bands are reported unscaled and carry a measured systematic of 20 to 30 cm−1
  from the two S–S anchors that bracket the fingerprint window, the strongest Raman mode of the S8 crown
  (implied factor 0.94) and the S3•− stretch measured by resonance Raman in TEGDME
  solutions (0.959). For 30 of the 37 species in DMSO the dominant Raman band falls between 395 and
  585 cm−1, a window free of strong electrolyte interference.


  
Per-species spectra

  
Every normal mode of the selected species as a stick, with a Lorentzian envelope, in one or
  both solvents (DMSO solid, TEGDME dashed), under either frequency convention. Hover a stick for its
  frequency in both conventions, its activity and its symmetry label; the table below lists every mode and
  both downloads work offline.

  

    
    
    
    
    
    
  

  

    
    

  

  

  
Sticks: harmonic IR intensity (km/mol) or Raman activity (Å⁴/amu) of each mode. Envelope:
  sum of Lorentzians whose peak equals each stick, so an isolated band reads as its activity. The paper's
  synthetic spectra use 638 nm scattering intensities rather than activities. ← / → step through species.

  

    
    
  

  


  
Dominant bands (cm−1)
